# Florida PIP Coverage Explained: Current 2026 Guide

**Correction updated June 30, 2026:** Florida PIP coverage has **not** been repealed. PIP remains required for most Florida-registered vehicles with four or more wheels.

## Current Florida PIP Requirements

Florida's current minimum auto insurance requirements still include:

- **$10,000 Personal Injury Protection (PIP)**
- **$10,000 Property Damage Liability (PDL)**

PIP is Florida's no-fault coverage. It helps pay covered injury-related expenses after an auto accident regardless of who caused the crash, subject to policy terms and Florida law.

## What PIP Generally Covers

Florida PIP coverage may include:

- A portion of covered medical expenses up to the policy limit.
- A portion of lost wages if accident injuries prevent you from working.
- Certain replacement services.
- Death benefits in qualifying fatal accidents.

The standard minimum limit is $10,000. Serious accidents can exceed that amount quickly, which is why many drivers review additional coverage options.

## Key PIP Rules Florida Drivers Should Know

### The 14-Day Rule

Florida has a strict timing rule for PIP claims. Initial medical services and care must generally begin within 14 days after the motor vehicle accident to qualify for PIP benefits.

### Emergency Medical Condition Limits

Access to the full $10,000 PIP limit may depend on whether a qualified provider determines that the injury is an emergency medical condition. Without that determination, benefits may be limited.

### PIP Does Not Replace Liability Protection

PIP helps with your own covered injury expenses. It does not replace Bodily Injury Liability coverage, which can help protect you if you injure someone else in an at-fault accident.

## Did Florida PIP End in July 2026?

No. Florida PIP did not end in July 2026 under current law.

HB 1181 was a 2025 proposal that would have changed Florida's no-fault system, but it did not become law. The official Florida Senate bill page lists it as **Died in Judiciary Committee**.

That means the current PIP and PDL requirements remain in place.

## Why You May Still Want a Coverage Review

Even though the July 2026 PIP-ending claim is inaccurate, a review can still be valuable. Minimum coverage is often only a starting point.

A good auto policy review should look at:

- PIP and PDL limits.
- Bodily Injury Liability limits.
- Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist coverage.
- Vehicles and drivers in the household.
- Deductibles and out-of-pocket exposure.
- Any motorcycles, trailers, RVs, or specialty vehicles that need separate handling.

## Common Questions

### Is PIP still required in Florida?

Yes. PIP remains required for most Florida-registered vehicles with four or more wheels.

### Did HB 1181 become law?

No. HB 1181 died in committee and did not become law.

### Do I need Bodily Injury Liability coverage?

It may be strongly recommended depending on your situation. It is separate from PIP and can help protect you if you cause injury to someone else.

### Is Uninsured Motorist coverage required?

No, but Florida insurers must offer it. It is worth reviewing carefully before rejecting it.
