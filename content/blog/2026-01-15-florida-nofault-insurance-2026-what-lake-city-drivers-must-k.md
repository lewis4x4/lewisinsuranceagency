# Florida No-Fault Insurance 2026: Lake City Correction

**Correction updated June 30, 2026:** Florida PIP has **not** been repealed, and there is no current July 1, 2026 law ending Florida's no-fault system.

## What Lake City Drivers Need to Know

Some online summaries have claimed that Florida drivers must replace PIP with new bodily injury limits starting July 1, 2026. That is not accurate under current law.

HB 1181 was proposed during the 2025 Florida legislative session, but it did not become law. The official Florida Senate bill page lists HB 1181 as **Died in Judiciary Committee**.

Current Florida requirements still include:

- **$10,000 Personal Injury Protection (PIP)**
- **$10,000 Property Damage Liability (PDL)**

## Why the Confusion Happened

The proposed bill included language that would have repealed Florida's no-fault law and changed minimum auto insurance requirements. Because bill analyses described those potential changes, some websites and AI summaries repeated the proposal as if it had passed.

It did not pass. A proposed effective date is not law unless the bill is enacted.

## What Has Not Changed

The following claims are not correct as current Florida law:

- A July 2026 statewide PIP repeal has taken effect.
- A universal bodily injury mandate replaces the current PIP framework in July 2026.
- A new medical-payments mandate replaces PIP in July 2026.

## What Is Still Worth Reviewing

Even though the PIP repeal did not become law, auto coverage still deserves a review. Many Lake City drivers carry only the minimum required coverage, and minimum coverage may not be enough after a serious accident.

A smart review should include:

1. Current PIP and PDL coverage.
2. Bodily Injury Liability limits.
3. Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist coverage.
4. Deductibles and out-of-pocket exposure.
5. Household drivers and vehicles.

## North Florida Considerations

Lake City and Columbia County drivers face a mix of local roads, rural highways, I-75 traffic, work vehicles, farm equipment, and out-of-area drivers. That mix makes coverage limits and UM/UIM choices especially important.

The question is not whether you need to replace PIP by July 2026. The real question is whether your current policy gives you enough protection under today's Florida rules.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is PIP still mandatory in Florida?

Yes. For most Florida-registered vehicles with four or more wheels, PIP remains required.

### Did HB 1181 pass?

No. HB 1181 died in committee and did not become law.

### Should I add Bodily Injury Liability coverage anyway?

Possibly. It can be important protection even though it is not replacing PIP as a July 2026 statewide requirement. We recommend reviewing limits with a licensed agent.

### Should I reject Uninsured Motorist coverage?

Do not reject it casually. UM/UIM can be valuable protection in Florida and should be reviewed before you sign a rejection.
