# Florida Workers Comp Rates: 6.9% Cut Already in Force for 2026. NCCI Proposes Another for 2027.

If you run a business in Lake City, Columbia County, or elsewhere in North Florida, workers compensation pricing is finally moving in employers' favor again. Two different things are true at once. Florida already has an **approved** statewide rate decrease in force for **2026**. Separately, the National Council on Compensation Insurance (NCCI) has **filed a proposed** further cut for **2027** that is **not** approved yet.

Treat those as two clocks. One is live on new and renewal policies today. The other is still sitting with the Florida Office of Insurance Regulation (OIR).

## What OIR already approved for 2026

On Nov. 17, 2025, Florida Insurance Commissioner Mike Yaworsky issued a Final Order approving a statewide overall **6.9%** decrease for workers compensation policies. The reduction, proposed by NCCI, applies to **new and renewal** policies effective in Florida starting **Jan. 1, 2026**. OIR's announcement and GovDelivery bulletin both call this the **ninth consecutive year** of Florida workers compensation rate decreases.

OIR's 2025 NCCI public rate hearing page records that NCCI asked for that same overall **6.9%** voluntary-market decrease for policies effective Jan. 1, 2026, before the Final Order issued.

A Jan. 23, 2026 actuarial peer review prepared for FLOIR summarizes how that statewide **-6.9%** was allocated across industry groups in the 2026 filing (not a quote for any one employer):

- Manufacturing: **-5.6%**
- Contracting: **-8.9%**
- Office & Clerical: **-9.5%**
- Goods & Services: **-5.8%**
- Miscellaneous: **-5.3%**
- Overall: **-6.9%**

Your class codes, payroll, experience modification, and credits still decide the premium on your policy. A statewide average is not a guarantee that every Lake City employer sees the same percentage.

## What NCCI filed for 2027 (still proposed)

In late August 2026, trade coverage (including Business Insurance on Aug. 24, 2026, plus Orlando Weekly and Insurance Business) reported that NCCI filed a **proposed average 7.4%** Florida workers compensation rate decrease for the voluntary market, aimed at policies effective **Jan. 1, 2027**.

That filing is a **recommendation**. OIR still runs the hearing and Final Order process. Coverage as of early September 2026 notes that a public hearing date for the 2027 filing had **not** been posted yet. Do not price a 2027 renewal as if **7.4%** is already law.

## What to check before your renewal

1. **Policy effective date.** Confirm whether you are still on a 2025 rate level, already on 2026 rates, or looking ahead to a 2027 renewal.
2. **Class codes and payroll.** Wrong codes or stale payroll estimates can erase a statewide decrease before it shows up on the invoice.
3. **Experience modification.** Your mod can move premium up or down independent of the statewide filing.
4. **Credits and schedule rating.** Ask what is already applied and what still needs underwriting support.
5. **Watch OIR for the 2027 hearing.** Notices typically post on [floir.gov](https://floir.gov/). Until a Final Order issues, treat **7.4%** as proposed only.
