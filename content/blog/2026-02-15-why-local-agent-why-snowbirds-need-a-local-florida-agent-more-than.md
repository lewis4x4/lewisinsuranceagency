This scenario isn't fiction. It's happening right now across Florida. Citizens Property Insurance Corporation rate changes for 2026 vary by policy and location. But here's what your out-of-state agent might not tell you: if your Florida home isn't your primary residence (Florida law defines that as a home you live in more than nine months a year), Citizens eligibility works differently than many snowbirds expect. When an authorized insurer, or an approved surplus lines insurer through Citizens' take-out program, offers comparable coverage, you lose Citizens eligibility unless that offer costs more than 20% above Citizens' premium. In other words, a private offer can cost up to 20% more than Citizens and you may still have to move.

When you buy insurance from a 1-800 number or online platform, you're getting generic advice from representatives who've never experienced Florida's unique challenges. They don't know that Florida law (s. 627.7011(5), Florida Statutes) bars an insurer from refusing to issue or renew a homeowner's policy solely because the roof is less than 15 years old. If your roof is 15 or older, the insurer must let you get an inspection by an authorized inspector, at your own cost, before requiring replacement. If that inspection shows at least 5 years of useful life left, the insurer can't refuse or nonrenew based on roof age alone. These protections apply to homeowners' policies, not mobile home policies, and some seasonal homes are insured on other policy forms. Ask us which form your home is on. They can't explain that many seasonal policies have unoccupancy or vacancy conditions, such as regular property checks or water shut-off rules, that can affect a claim if you're gone for months. These representatives change constantly, meaning you'll explain your snowbird situation to a different person every time you call.
