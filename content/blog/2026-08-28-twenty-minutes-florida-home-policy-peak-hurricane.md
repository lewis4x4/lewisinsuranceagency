# Take Twenty Minutes With Your Florida Home Policy Before Peak Season

Late August is peak hurricane season in Florida. You do not need a weekend project. You need twenty quiet minutes with your declarations page and a phone camera before the next named storm shows up on the map.

This is not another full prep checklist. It is the insurance half of readiness: deductible, flood timing, and proof of what you own. When you want the longer home and evacuation list, use our [Florida hurricane preparation checklist](https://lewisinsurance.com/hurricane-preparation-checklist).

## 1. Find your hurricane deductible

On most Florida homeowners policies, the hurricane deductible is a percent of dwelling coverage, not the same flat dollar amount you see for other claims. Pull the declarations page and write down:

- The hurricane or windstorm deductible percentage (or dollar amount, if that is how yours is written)
- The dwelling Coverage A limit it applies to
- What that means in dollars for your home

If a named storm damages the house during the hurricane deductible period, that number is your out-of-pocket before the carrier pays covered wind losses. Knowing it now beats learning it after the power is out.

## 2. Check flood coverage timing

Standard homeowners policies do not cover flood. Flood is separate. Through the National Flood Insurance Program, a new flood policy usually has about a 30-day waiting period before it takes effect. There are narrow exceptions (for example, some loan closings and certain map changes), but waiting until a storm is already in the Gulf is the wrong plan for that storm.

If you need flood coverage and do not have it, start the conversation while the weather is calm. For North Florida context, see our [flood insurance guides](https://lewisinsurance.com/blog) and talk with our team about NFIP or private flood options that fit the property.

## 3. Snap a calm-day home inventory

Walk every room. Photograph walls, floors, closets, big ticket items, and serial numbers where you can see them. Save the photos to cloud storage you can reach from a phone if you evacuate.

A clear inventory speeds a claim and reduces guesswork. If you want a structured worksheet, use our [home inventory checklist](https://lewisinsurance.com/home-inventory-checklist).

## 4. Put documents where you can reach them

Keep digital copies of your declarations page, flood declarations (if any), agent contact card, and mortgage company info. Paper in a drawer does not help if you leave in a hurry.
