# Florida Roof Insurance Requirements 2026: New Laws and Rules Every Homeowner Needs to Know

Florida roof insurance requirements keep evolving, bringing both protection and new challenges for homeowners across the Sunshine State. Florida law already limits when an insurer can drop a home over roof age alone. That rule has been in place since 2022 ([s. 627.7011(5), F.S.](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/627.7011); [ch. 2022-268, Laws of Fla.](https://laws.flrules.org/2022/268)). A 2026 bill to expand it, HB 815, [died in committee](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026/815) and did not become law.

If you're a Florida homeowner, understanding these requirements can help you protect your coverage from unexpected cancellation. Let's dive into everything you need to know about Florida's evolving roof insurance landscape.

## Florida's 15-Year Roof Rule: What It Means for You

The 15-Year Rule: For homeowners' policies issued or renewed on or after July 1, 2022, an insurer may not refuse to issue or renew solely because the roof is less than 15 years old ([s. 627.7011(5)(b), (e)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/627.7011)).

This landmark legislation addresses one of the most frustrating aspects of Florida's insurance crisis. In the past, even if your roof was perfectly sound and leak-free, an insurance carrier could drop your coverage simply because it hit a "magic number" (often just 10 or 15 years for shingles).

### Key Protections Under s. 627.7011(5)

**For Roofs Under 15 Years:**
- Complete protection from age-based cancellation

**For Roofs 15+ Years Old:**
The "Useful Life" Extension: For roofs 15 years or older, the insurer must allow an inspection at the homeowner's expense to prove the roof's viability. If an authorized inspector determines your roof has at least 5 years of useful life remaining, the insurer cannot drop you based on age alone.

### Authorized Inspectors

Who counts as an authorized inspector is set in s. 627.7011(5)(a): a licensed home inspector, certified building code inspector, licensed general, building or residential contractor or a roofing contractor, licensed engineer, licensed architect, or another inspector the insurer recognizes. Roofing contractors were added in 2024 ([ch. 2024-182](https://laws.flrules.org/2024/182)).

## Building Code Requirements That Affect Insurance

Florida's building codes directly impact your insurance coverage. Your roof needs to withstand wind speeds up to 150 miles per hour in most areas. If your roof doesn't meet these standards, you could face problems when it's time to file an insurance claim.

### Approved Materials and Standards

Common approved materials include asphalt shingles, metal roofing, and clay tiles. Concrete tiles also work well. The key is that whatever material you choose must be rated for Florida's climate conditions.

For Lake City and North Florida residents, these requirements are especially relevant given our region's exposure to severe weather patterns and occasional hurricane impacts.

### Permit and Inspection Requirements

Any significant roof work in Florida requires a permit from your local building department. This includes new roofs, reroofs, and major repairs. Unpermitted work can cause serious problems when you sell your home or file insurance claims.

## What Happens If You Receive a Non-Renewal Notice

If you receive a non-renewal notice citing roof age in 2026, don't panic. Florida's roof-age rule provides specific steps you can take:

### Your Action Plan

Verify the Age: Calculate the age from the last date 100% of the roof's surface was built or replaced, or the initial date of a partial replacement that later reached 100%.

Order an Independent Inspection: Hire a licensed home inspector or roofing contractor to perform a Roof Condition Certification.

Submit Proof of "Useful Life": If the report shows 5+ years of life, your insurer is legally barred from dropping you solely due to age under Florida Statute 627.7011.

Consult an Agent: Work with a local agent who understands Florida's roof-age rules.

## Why the Rule Matters

Under this rule, an insurer cannot refuse to issue or renew a homeowner's policy solely because of roof age when the roof is less than 15 years old, or when an authorized inspection shows at least 5 years of useful life remaining ([s. 627.7011(5)(b), (c)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/627.7011)). That can spare families who maintain their roofs from replacing a functional roof just to keep insurance.

Metal & Tile Advantage: Since metal and tile roofs often last 30–50 years, this law prevents carriers from arbitrarily applying "shingle-life" logic to superior materials.

## Special Considerations for North Florida Homeowners

Living in the Lake City area brings unique advantages and considerations for roof insurance requirements:

**Climate Factors:**
- Lower hurricane risk compared to coastal areas
- Reduced salt air exposure extends roof life
- Potential for severe thunderstorms and tornadoes

**Building Materials:**
For our region, metal roofs last 40 to 70 years, far longer than asphalt shingles. Metal roofs have excellent wind resistance. Insurance companies often give discounts for metal roofs.

## How Wind Mitigation Can Save You Money

Hip roofs typically perform better in high winds and may qualify for credits versus gable designs. Improved fasteners (e.g., ring-shank nails) and proper spacing help keep decking secured during storms. Underlayment that seals the deck offers extra protection if shingles are compromised. Hurricane clips/straps that tie the roof system to wall studs can unlock meaningful credits.

A certified wind-mitigation inspection verifies your features so carriers can apply the credits.

## The 25% Rule and When It Applies

Florida's 25% rule remains in effect and continues to trigger full code compliance when repair work exceeds one-quarter of the roof area within a 12-month period.

This rule is particularly important for homeowners considering partial roof repairs versus full replacement. Understanding this threshold can help you make informed decisions about maintenance and repairs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Florida's 15-year roof rule?
It prevents insurers from refusing to cover a home solely because the roof is under 15 years old. For roofs 15+ years old, homeowners can get an inspection to show 5+ years of remaining life.

### Can my insurance be canceled because my roof is old?
Yes, but only if the roof is 15+ years old and does not pass a certified inspection showing sufficient remaining life.

### Who can inspect my roof for insurance in Florida?
An inspector approved by your insurer who is a licensed home inspector, certified building code inspector, licensed contractor or roofing contractor, licensed engineer or architect, or another inspector the insurer recognizes (s. 627.7011(5)(a)). HB 1611 (ch. 2024-182) added roofing contractors.

### Does the 25% rule still apply?
Yes, Florida's 25% rule remains in effect and continues to trigger full code compliance when repair work exceeds one-quarter of the roof area within a 12-month period.

### Did Florida pass a new roof-age law in 2026?
No. HB 815 and SB 808 died in committee in March 2026. The current 15-year rule in s. 627.7011(5) has applied to homeowners' policies issued or renewed on or after July 1, 2022.
