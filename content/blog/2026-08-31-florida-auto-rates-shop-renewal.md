# Florida Auto Rates Are Moving Lower in 2026. Why Lake City Drivers Should Shop at Renewal

For a few years, Florida auto insurance felt like one direction only: up. That story is changing. In 2026, the Florida Office of Insurance Regulation (FLOIR) has been public about large writers filing decreases. If you drive in Lake City or elsewhere in North Florida, the useful move is not to guess what you will save. It is to shop again when your renewal arrives.

## What regulators actually said

On March 5, 2026, Commissioner Mike Yaworsky announced that Florida's top five personal auto writer groups were indicating an average rate change of about **-8% for 2026**. Those groups are Progressive, Berkshire Hathaway (GEICO), State Farm, Allstate, and USAA. Together they amount to about **78% of Florida's auto market**, per FLOIR.

Earlier that year, OIR approved more carrier-specific cuts. A January 28, 2026 FLOIR release notes that **USAA filed an average 7% decrease** scheduled to take effect by May 2026, and that dozens of personal-auto writers had filed decreases over the prior year. Trade coverage in August 2026 (Insurance Business, citing FLOIR) kept the same theme: more filings, including additional GEICO decreases.

None of that is a promise about *your* premium. Filings are company-specific. Timing, territory, drivers, vehicles, and coverage choices still matter. What it does mean is the old "rates only go up" assumption is a bad way to renew in 2026.

## Why renewal is the moment that matters

A rate filing is not a mid-term coupon every driver gets overnight. Your current term usually runs until the end date on the declarations page. Shopping at renewal (or when you are free to move) is when competing filings can show up in a real quote.

Sitting on last year's number can leave better options on the table even when the statewide headlines look friendly. The reverse is also true: a cut at one company does not force every competitor to match it on your driveway in Columbia County.

## A practical Lake City shopping checklist

Before you call for quotes, pull last year's paperwork and spend ten quiet minutes:

1. **Declarations page.** Note liability limits, comprehensive and collision deductibles, drivers listed, and vehicles.
2. **Who is really on the policy.** Add or remove drivers the honest way. A missing teen or a roommate who borrows the truck will show up later.
3. **Miles and use.** Commute, pleasure, and business use change pricing. Update what is true now.
4. **Compare coverage, not just price.** Florida has minimum liability requirements, but many families carry higher limits because a serious crash costs more than the state floor. Match apples to apples across quotes.
5. **Ask about packaging.** If you also insure a home or renters policy, ask whether a multi-policy discount applies. Do not invent a savings number. Just ask.
6. **Re-shop when the market moves.** If your renewal jumps, or a carrier you like announces another Florida cut, ask for a fresh market check.
