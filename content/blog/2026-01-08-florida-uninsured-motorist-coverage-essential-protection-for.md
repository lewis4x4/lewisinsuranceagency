# Florida Uninsured Motorist Coverage: Essential Protection for 2026

Uninsured Motorist (UM) and Underinsured Motorist (UIM) coverage can be one of the most important choices on a Florida auto policy.

**Correction updated June 30, 2026:** Florida PIP has not been repealed. Claims assigning PIP a July 1, 2026 termination date were based on proposed legislation that did not become law.

## Why Florida Drivers Review UM/UIM Coverage

Florida's minimum required coverage may not be enough after a serious accident, and some drivers carry no coverage at all. UM/UIM coverage helps protect you when the at-fault driver cannot pay for the injuries they cause.

This can matter after:

- Hit-and-run accidents.
- Crashes with uninsured drivers.
- Crashes with drivers carrying low limits.
- Serious injury claims that exceed the at-fault driver's coverage.

## Current Florida Requirements

Current Florida minimum requirements still include:

- **$10,000 Personal Injury Protection (PIP)**
- **$10,000 Property Damage Liability (PDL)**

PIP remains part of Florida's no-fault framework. UM/UIM is optional, but insurers must offer it.

## The 2026 Correction

HB 1181 proposed changes to Florida's no-fault law, but it did not pass. The official Florida Senate bill page lists the bill as **Died in Judiciary Committee**.

That means there is no current July 2026 law eliminating PIP or replacing it with mandatory 25/50 bodily injury limits.

## Stacked vs. Non-Stacked Coverage

Florida offers stacked and non-stacked UM/UIM options. Stacked coverage can allow limits to combine across multiple vehicles on a policy. Non-stacked coverage is usually less expensive but more limited.

Families with more than one vehicle should compare both options before making a decision.

## Local Considerations for Lake City Drivers

North Florida drivers face a mix of rural roads, I-75 and I-10 traffic, work trucks, agricultural vehicles, and out-of-area drivers. That makes coverage limits and UM/UIM decisions worth reviewing carefully.

## Action Steps

1. Review your declarations page.
2. Confirm whether UM/UIM is included or rejected.
3. Compare stacked and non-stacked options if you have multiple vehicles.
4. Review Bodily Injury Liability and PDL limits.
5. Ask a licensed agent to separate legal requirements from optional protection.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is uninsured motorist coverage required in Florida?

No. It is optional, but insurance companies must offer it and you must reject it in writing if you do not want it.

### Did PIP ending in 2026 make UM mandatory?

No. PIP has not ended under current law, and UM/UIM has not become mandatory statewide.

### Is UM still recommended?

For many Florida drivers, yes. It can be important protection against uninsured, underinsured, and hit-and-run drivers.
