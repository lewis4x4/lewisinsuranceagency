A Florida homeowners renewal is usually more than a bill. The packet can include a renewal-premium notice, a separate "Notice of Change in Policy Terms," or, if the company is leaving you, a nonrenewal or cancellation notice. Those are different documents with different timing. This is a reading guide, not legal advice.

Start with the title on the page. Florida law treats residential property (homeowners, many condo and mobile-home owner policies, and similar residential forms) under special notice rules. For those policies, the insurer generally has to send the first-named insured a written renewal-premium notice at least 45 days ahead. A nonrenewal, cancellation, or termination notice is generally at least 120 days ahead and has to state a reason, with listed exceptions. Nonpayment is a shorter clock, often 10 days. Do not treat a 10-day nonpay letter like a 120-day nonrenewal.

On the renewal-premium notice, look for the dollars the statute says must be spelled out. The notice has to show amounts recouped for assessments by the Florida Hurricane Catastrophe Fund, Citizens Property Insurance Corporation, and the Florida Insurance Guaranty Association, using those names next to the dollars. It also has to show how much of a premium increase is an approved rate change and how much is from coverage changes. That is why the page can look busy. Those lines are not a Citizens eligibility guide. They are a breakdown of this year's premium.

If the company is changing terms, look for a page titled exactly "Notice of Change in Policy Terms." Florida law defines a change in policy terms as a modification, addition, or deletion of a term, coverage, duty, or condition. A typo fix is not that. A change the legislature required is not that. If terms do change, the insurer has to give advance written notice summarizing the change. The notice can ride with the renewal-premium notice, or it can go separately on the nonrenewal-notice timeframe for that line. Since January 1, 2025, that change notice has to be in bold type of at least 14 points and on a single page or consecutive pages.

Optional new coverage that raises the premium cannot be added through that notice unless you affirmatively approve it. A change to the base policy, a deductible, or a limit is not "optional coverage" under that rule.

Paying the renewal premium is treated as accepting the new policy terms. If the required change notice was not given, the original terms stay in effect until the next renewal with proper notice, or until you have replacement coverage, whichever comes first. If other required notice (other than the 10-day nonpay notice) is late, coverage can stay in force for a limited period set out in the statute. That is a reason to call, not a reason to ignore the packet.
