# NFIP Authorization Ends Sept. 30: What North Florida Homeowners and Buyers Should Do Now

Congress renews the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) on a set schedule. FEMA says current authorization runs through September 30, 2026. That date matters for Lake City and North Florida homeowners, buyers, and anyone with a mortgage in a flood zone. Your homeowners policy is not flood insurance. Flood needs its own policy.

## What expires on September 30

On February 3, 2026, the president signed legislation extending NFIP authorization to September 30, 2026. FEMA's February 4, 2026 update says Congress must reauthorize by no later than 11:59 p.m. that day.

What expires is the NFIP's authority to sell new flood policies and renew existing ones. It is not an automatic wipeout of every flood policy in force. The deadline is about Congress's authority to keep writing new NFIP contracts past that night.

## What stays in force if Congress misses the date

If authorization lapses, FEMA says it would stop selling and renewing NFIP policies. FEMA also says it would still pay valid claims with available funds, and that the agency and Congress have never failed to honor flood contracts already in place.

The National Association of Realtors (NAR) FAQ matches that picture:

- No new NFIP policies and no NFIP renewals until reauthorization.
- Existing NFIP policies stay in effect until their expiration date, including a 30-day grace period.
- Claims continue to be paid as long as FEMA has funds.
- Private flood insurance (not backed by the NFIP) is not affected.
- Many federal lending regulators suspend the flood insurance purchase requirement during a lapse. Lenders still decide whether to make loans in special flood hazard areas while NFIP is unavailable.

NAR also notes an insurer may sometimes assign a seller's NFIP policy to a buyer by substituting names. Confirm that with your lender, title company, and agent. It does not replace knowing your renewal date.

## Who feels a lapse first

Buyers, refinancers, and anyone whose NFIP renewal falls near the deadline feel a lapse first. A federally related mortgage that needs flood insurance can stall if NFIP cannot issue a new policy. A renewal during a lapse cannot go through NFIP until Congress acts again.

FEMA's page cites a nationwide NAR estimate that a lapse might affect about 1,300 property sales each day, or roughly 40,000 closings per month. That is a national figure, not Florida-only.

If you already hold an in-force NFIP policy with time left on the term, a lapse does not cancel that coverage overnight. It continues for its remaining term.

## A practical checklist for Lake City and North Florida clients

1. **Pull your flood declarations page.** Note the policy number, effective dates, and whether it is NFIP or private flood.
2. **Mark your renewal date.** If it falls in late September or early October, talk with us before the window gets tight.
3. **Do not assume homeowners covers flood.** Ask whether you need a separate flood policy.
4. **Buying or refinancing?** Ask your lender early what flood proof they will accept if NFIP sales pause. Private flood may still be an option; read any private policy carefully.
5. **Keep claim contacts handy.** FEMA says valid claims can still be paid with available funds during a lapse.
