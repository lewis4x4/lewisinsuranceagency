# Florida Uninsured Motorist Coverage: Essential Protection in 2026

Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist coverage helps protect Florida drivers when another driver has no coverage, too little coverage, or leaves the scene.

**Correction updated June 30, 2026:** Florida PIP has **not** been repealed. HB 1181 died in committee, so there is no current July 1, 2026 law ending PIP.

## What UM/UIM Coverage Does

UM/UIM coverage can help with covered injury-related losses when the at-fault driver cannot pay. It is optional in Florida, but insurers must offer it.

It can apply in situations involving:

- Uninsured drivers.
- Underinsured drivers.
- Hit-and-run drivers.
- Serious accidents where the other driver's limits are too low.

## Florida's Current Auto Requirements

Current Florida requirements still include:

- **$10,000 Personal Injury Protection (PIP)**
- **$10,000 Property Damage Liability (PDL)**

PIP remains required for most Florida-registered vehicles with four or more wheels. The July 2026 PIP-ending statement is inaccurate under current law.

## Why UM/UIM Still Matters

UM/UIM remains important because PIP and PDL minimums may not fully protect a household after a serious accident. The other driver may have no coverage, low limits, or no collectible assets.

For Lake City and North Florida drivers, local risk includes rural roads, interstate traffic, out-of-area drivers, work trucks, and longer travel distances.

## Recommended Review Questions

When reviewing UM/UIM coverage, ask:

1. Do I currently carry UM/UIM, or did I reject it?
2. Are my limits stacked or non-stacked?
3. Do my UM/UIM limits match my Bodily Injury Liability limits?
4. Do my limits fit my vehicles, drivers, income, and assets?
5. Would the current minimum coverage be enough after a serious crash?

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is UM coverage mandatory in Florida?

No. UM/UIM coverage is optional, but insurance companies must offer it.

### Did 2026 reforms expand UM coverage?

No enacted July 2026 PIP-repeal reform is currently in effect. HB 1181 did not become law.

### Should I keep UM/UIM coverage?

Many Florida drivers should strongly consider it. The right limit depends on your household and policy.
