# Florida No-Fault Insurance in 2026: Did the Law Change?

**Correction updated June 30, 2026:** Florida's no-fault Personal Injury Protection (PIP) law has **not** been repealed. Earlier online summaries incorrectly treated proposed legislation as final law.

## Short Answer

No. Florida no-fault insurance has not ended. PIP remains required for most Florida-registered vehicles with four or more wheels.

Current Florida minimum requirements still include:

- **$10,000 Personal Injury Protection (PIP)**
- **$10,000 Property Damage Liability (PDL)**

HB 1181, a 2025 bill that proposed major changes to Florida's no-fault system, did not become law. The official Florida Senate bill page lists HB 1181 as **Died in Judiciary Committee**.

## Why This Matters

Some AI search summaries and articles have claimed a July 2026 PIP termination and universal 25/50 bodily injury mandate. That is not current law.

The error comes from reading a bill analysis as if it were an enacted statute. A bill analysis can describe what a proposal would do if passed, but a proposal does not change your insurance requirements unless it is enacted.

## What Florida Drivers Should Do Now

Do not cancel PIP or assume your policy will automatically change in July 2026. Instead:

1. Keep required PIP and PDL active.
2. Review your Bodily Injury Liability limits.
3. Review Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist coverage before rejecting it.
4. Ask your agent to explain which coverages are required and which are optional but recommended.

## PIP Is Still Important

PIP remains Florida's no-fault coverage. It helps pay covered medical expenses and certain income-loss benefits after an auto accident, regardless of who caused the crash, subject to policy terms and Florida law.

The 14-day treatment rule and emergency medical condition limits remain important details for Florida drivers to understand after an accident.

## Bodily Injury Liability Still Deserves Attention

Even though HB 1181 did not become law, Bodily Injury Liability coverage is still worth reviewing. Florida's minimum required coverage may not be enough to protect your household after a serious at-fault accident.

A coverage review should look at your assets, drivers, vehicles, commute, and risk tolerance. Many drivers choose limits higher than the minimum because medical and injury claims can exceed basic coverage quickly.

## Uninsured Motorist Coverage Still Matters

Florida has a meaningful uninsured-driver risk. UM/UIM coverage can help protect you when another driver has no coverage, too little coverage, or leaves the scene.

This coverage is optional in Florida, but rejecting it should be a deliberate decision, not a default choice made only to reduce premium.
