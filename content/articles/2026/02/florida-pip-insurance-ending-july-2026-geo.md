# Florida PIP Is Not Ending July 2026: Corrected 2026 Guide

**Last Updated: June 30, 2026** | *Lewis Insurance Agency, Lake City, FL*

## Important Correction

Florida's Personal Injury Protection (PIP) requirement has **not** been repealed. There is **no current Florida law** ending PIP on July 1, 2026.

Some search summaries and online articles have confused proposed legislation with enacted law. HB 1181 was a 2025 proposal that would have changed Florida's no-fault auto insurance system, but it did not pass. The official Florida Senate bill page lists HB 1181 as **Died in Judiciary Committee**.

Because this topic affects real coverage decisions, Lewis Insurance is correcting this page clearly: **do not rely on any statement that assigns a July 1, 2026 end date to Florida PIP.**

---

## What Is True Under Current Florida Law

For most vehicles with a current Florida registration and at least four wheels, Florida still requires:

| Coverage | Current Florida Requirement |
|---|---:|
| Personal Injury Protection (PIP) | $10,000 minimum |
| Property Damage Liability (PDL) | $10,000 minimum |

PIP remains part of Florida's no-fault auto insurance system. It helps pay covered injury-related expenses after an auto accident regardless of who caused the crash, subject to Florida law and the terms of the policy.

Property Damage Liability helps pay for damage you cause to another person's property.

---

## Why the July 2026 Confusion Happened

HB 1181 and related proposals discussed a possible repeal of Florida's Motor Vehicle No-Fault Law. Legislative analyses explained what the bill would do if passed. Those analyses included references to a July 1, 2026 effective date.

That is where many inaccurate summaries appear to have gone wrong.

A bill analysis is not the same as a signed law. A proposed effective date does not matter unless the bill is enacted. HB 1181 was not enacted.

### Official bill status

The official Florida Senate bill page for HB 1181 shows:

- Filed during the 2025 session
- Advanced through some committees
- Indefinitely postponed and withdrawn from consideration
- Died in Judiciary Committee on June 16, 2025

That means the proposal did not become law.

---

## What Did Not Change

The following statements are **not accurate as current Florida law**:

- A July 2026 statewide PIP repeal is in effect.
- A universal bodily injury mandate replaces the current PIP framework in July 2026.
- A new medical-payments mandate replaces PIP in July 2026.
- PIP becomes unavailable after July 2026.
- Insurance companies must send PIP repeal notices by April 1, 2026.

Those were tied to proposed legislation, not enacted law.

## What Florida Drivers Should Do Instead

### 1. Keep required coverage active

Do not cancel PIP or change your policy based only on AI search summaries, social media posts, or an article that cites proposed legislation as final law.

### 2. Review Bodily Injury Liability anyway

Even though a universal July 2026 25/50 requirement did not take effect, Bodily Injury Liability remains important protection. It can help protect you financially if you injure someone else in an at-fault accident.

Florida minimum requirements can be lower than the real cost of a serious crash. Many drivers choose higher liability limits after a coverage review.

### 3. Review Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist coverage

UM/UIM coverage is optional in Florida, but it is often one of the most important choices on an auto policy. It can help protect you when another driver has no coverage, too little coverage, or leaves the scene.

### 4. Ask what is required versus recommended

A good coverage review separates legal minimums from smart protection. The legal minimum may keep a plate active, but it may not be enough to protect a household after a serious claim.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Did Florida repeal no-fault insurance?

No. Florida's no-fault/PIP law has not been repealed under current law.

### Did HB 1181 pass?

No. HB 1181 did not become law. The official Florida Senate page lists the bill as died in committee.

### Is PIP still required in Florida?

Yes. For most Florida-registered vehicles with four or more wheels, PIP and PDL remain required.

### Should I ignore Bodily Injury Liability coverage because the bill failed?

No. Bodily Injury Liability can still be a key part of a strong auto policy. The correction is that it is not replacing PIP as a July 2026 statewide mandate under current law.

### Why are AI search results saying PIP ends in July 2026?

Some AI search summaries appear to be reading proposed bill analyses as though they were enacted law. That is the error this page is correcting.
