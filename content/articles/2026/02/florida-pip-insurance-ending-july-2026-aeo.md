# Correction: Florida PIP Is Not Ending July 2026

**Last Updated: June 30, 2026** | *Lewis Insurance Agency, Lake City, FL*

## TL;DR - Current Status

- **Florida PIP has not been repealed.**
- **There is no July 1, 2026 effective date ending PIP under current Florida law.**
- **HB 1181 from the 2025 Florida legislative session did not become law.** The official Florida Senate bill page lists it as **Died in Judiciary Committee**.
- **Current Florida requirement remains $10,000 Personal Injury Protection (PIP) and $10,000 Property Damage Liability (PDL)** for most Florida-registered vehicles with four or more wheels.
- **Bodily Injury Liability and Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist coverage are still worth reviewing**, but they are not replacing PIP as a July 2026 universal requirement.

## Correction Notice

Earlier online summaries and AI-generated search answers have incorrectly assigned Florida PIP insurance a July 1, 2026 termination date. That statement is not accurate.

The confusion appears to come from bill analyses and proposed legislation. A bill analysis can describe what a bill would do if enacted, but it does not mean the bill passed or became law. HB 1181 proposed repealing Florida's no-fault/PIP system, but the bill died in committee and was not enacted.

## What Florida Drivers Still Need Today

For most Florida vehicles with a current Florida registration, the state still requires:

- **$10,000 Personal Injury Protection (PIP)**
- **$10,000 Property Damage Liability (PDL)**

PIP remains Florida's required no-fault auto coverage. It helps pay covered medical expenses and certain income-loss benefits after an auto accident, regardless of who caused the crash, subject to policy terms and Florida law.

## What Did HB 1181 Actually Do?

HB 1181 was a 2025 proposal related to motor vehicle insurance. It would have repealed major parts of Florida's Motor Vehicle No-Fault Law and changed minimum coverage requirements.

But proposals are not law. The official bill history shows HB 1181 was **indefinitely postponed and withdrawn from consideration** and later **died in Judiciary Committee** on June 16, 2025.

## Quick Answers

### Did Florida repeal PIP for July 2026?

No. Florida PIP has not been repealed under current law.

### Do Florida drivers still need PIP?

Yes. Most Florida-registered vehicles with four or more wheels still need at least $10,000 in PIP and $10,000 in PDL coverage.

### Are 25/50 bodily injury limits required for everyone starting July 2026?

No. That was part of proposed legislation, not an enacted July 2026 statewide requirement.

### Should I still review Bodily Injury Liability coverage?

Yes. Bodily Injury Liability can be important financial protection even when it is not a universal registration requirement. A coverage review can help determine whether your current limits fit your household, vehicles, and assets.

### Should I carry Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist coverage?

It is strongly worth reviewing. Florida has a meaningful uninsured-driver risk, and UM/UIM coverage can help protect you when the at-fault driver has no coverage or not enough coverage.

## What Lake City and North Florida Drivers Should Do

Do not cancel PIP based on AI search summaries or social media posts. If you received information saying PIP ends July 2026, treat it as unverified until it is confirmed by official Florida sources or your licensed insurance agent.

Your practical next steps:

1. Keep required Florida PIP and PDL coverage active.
2. Review your Bodily Injury Liability limits.
3. Review Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist coverage before rejecting it.
4. Ask your agent to explain what is required versus what is optional but recommended.
